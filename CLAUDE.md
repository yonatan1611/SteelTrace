# SteelTrace

**Official title:** Reinforcement Steel Inventory and Traceability Management System

> **Provenance note:** SteelTrace is being built from ongoing discussions and observations with the client, not from a pre-existing formal specification. **Revision note (post data-model review 3):** earlier versions of this document used a single `[CONFIRMED]` tag for two genuinely different things — what the end client actually asked for, and what this project's own working sessions decided (e.g., building SteelTrace as a multi-tenant product). Those are now split apart. This document distinguishes five categories of statement throughout:
> - **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]** — stated by the actual end client (the construction company) about their operational needs. Traceable to the original business-requirements brief this project started from.
> - **[PRODUCT DECISION]** — decided in this project's own working sessions (architecture, data-integrity rules, building SteelTrace as a multi-tenant product, etc.) — a deliberate choice by the team building SteelTrace, not something the end client explicitly asked for. Carries real design weight, but must never be described as something the client confirmed.
> - **[PROPOSED]** — a design/technical recommendation from this team, not yet decided by anyone.
> - **[ASSUMPTION]** — a working assumption filling a gap, flagged for confirmation.
> - **[OPEN QUESTION]** — tracked in §8, not decided.
>
> Nothing marked PROPOSED or ASSUMPTION should be treated as final, and no open question should be silently treated as resolved. A `[PRODUCT DECISION]` should never be rephrased elsewhere as a client directive — if that happens, it's a documentation bug to fix, not a reflection of real client sign-off.

## 1. Project Overview

SteelTrace is a **general-purpose**, web-based system for construction companies to manage reinforcement steel inventory and trace the full lifecycle of steel materials — from supplier bundle, through warehouse storage, cutting/bending, offcut reuse, waste accounting, transportation, and final installation in a structural element. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**

Building it as a **multi-company (multi-tenant)** product — i.e., architected to serve more than one construction company, not only the first client — was decided in this project's working sessions. **[PRODUCT DECISION]**

The first deployment will serve one client on a hotel construction project, but the system must not be hardcoded to that project or that building type. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]** It must also not be hardcoded to that single company, support multiple concurrent construction projects per company, and support configurable construction-location hierarchies. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]** (the "per company" framing follows from the product decision above.)

Out of scope / explicitly rejected:
- No integration with the Turkish IDIS system. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- QR codes are generated by SteelTrace itself and reference only SteelTrace's own internal records. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- The system must never claim individual-bar-level traceability for material that has been physically mixed and can no longer be distinguished at that granularity. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- The system must never claim to know the exact identity of a physical bar when the real-world workflow cannot establish that identity. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**

## 2. Confirmed Requirements

### 2.1 Multi-Company Support (tenancy) **[PRODUCT DECISION]**
- SteelTrace must support multiple construction companies as isolated tenants. This was decided in this project's working sessions as a product-architecture goal (the first client did not ask for multi-tenancy), so that SteelTrace can serve future clients without a redesign.
- Per-company isolation is required for: users and permissions, projects, suppliers and manufacturers, bundles and inventory, processing operations, material genealogy, waste records, and reports.
- One company must never be able to access another company's private records, under any circumstance.
- The initial deployment may serve a single client, but the data model and authorization layer must not make multi-company support difficult to add later — tenancy must be a first-class concept from the start, not retrofitted.
- **Isolation is logical, not physical [PRODUCT DECISION]**: SteelTrace is one application on one shared database schema — there is no per-company table, schema, or database. Every tenant-scoped table carries a `company_id`, and isolation is enforced consistently on top of that shared structure (see `docs/data-model.md` §11 for the enforcement approach). This is a product/architecture decision made in this project's working sessions, not something the end client specified — the client cares that their data is private, not how that's implemented.
- Whether any data is intentionally shared *across* companies (e.g., a common catalog of standard rebar diameters/grades) is an open question — see §8. Default assumption until resolved: **no cross-company data sharing of any kind**, including reference/master data.

### 2.2 Bundle Registration **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Staff manually transcribe data from supplier/manufacturer paper labels: bundle reference, diameter, length, grade/quality, manufacturing standard, heat/casting number, piece count, supplier, manufacturer, country of origin, and other available specs (field set must be extensible — not all labels carry the same fields).
- Every registered bundle gets a unique **internal system identifier**, unique at least within its company.
- The system generates its own **QR code** per bundle, pointing at the internal record (no external scheme).

### 2.3 Inventory Management **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Track bundles and available steel quantities across multiple projects and storage locations, scoped to a single company at a time.
- Record incoming material, withdrawals, transfers between locations, and remaining quantities.
- Maintain a complete, accurate material movement history (append-only ledger semantics, not just current-state snapshots).

### 2.4 Material Traceability and Tracking Granularity **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Trace material from original bundle through withdrawal, processing, reuse, transportation, and installation.
- Preserve the relationship between source material and all derived material (genealogy graph, not a flat list).
- Support three traceability granularities, used as appropriate to the physical reality:
  1. **Bundle-level** — identity preserved only at the bundle.
  2. **Processing-batch-level** — the practical **default**. Identity is preserved for a batch of pieces produced together, without claiming to distinguish individual bars within that batch.
  3. **Individual-piece-level** — **optional, not mandatory.** Supported when a real-world workflow can actually establish individual identity (e.g., physically tagged/segregated pieces). Must never be applied to claim knowledge the real workflow doesn't support.
- The system must never retroactively upgrade a record's claimed granularity beyond what the recorded workflow actually supports.

### 2.5 Material Conservation and Reconciliation **[PRODUCT DECISION, formalizing a CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
The underlying need — that waste and reuse must be tracked accurately rather than approximated — is a client requirement (§2.9's "critical" offcut/waste distinction and the "calculate actual waste" goal from the original brief). The precise, formal invariant below (the 12 m example, the specific prevention list) was articulated in this project's working sessions, not dictated verbatim by the client, though it directly serves the client's stated need.

Material accounting must preserve quantities through every transformation: the sum of all recorded outputs of a transformation (used + reusable offcut + scrap + any other documented category) must reconcile against the recorded input, within any explicitly documented adjustment (e.g., measurement tolerance, correction).

Worked example:
- Input: 12 m.
- Outputs: 9 m prepared reinforcement + 2.5 m reusable offcut + 0.5 m actual scrap.
- Total accounted: 12 m. ✓

- **A single processing operation must support multiple source inputs** — e.g., 4 bars from Bundle A + 6 bars from Bundle B + 3 bars from Bundle C (13 bars total) consumed in one operation producing prepared reinforcement, reusable offcuts, and scrap. The contribution and origin of each input must be preserved, not flattened into an undifferentiated total.
- **Reconciliation is based on length (and weight, where actual weights are available) — not on piece count.** Piece count is expected to change across a cutting/bending operation (one bar becomes several pieces) and is not a valid balance check; length (and optionally weight) is. See `docs/data-model.md` §8 for how different piece counts, different lengths, multiple source materials, measurement tolerances, and unexplained differences are each handled.
- **Discrepancies must never silently disappear.** An operation whose outputs don't reconcile against its inputs (within a documented adjustment) must remain visibly flagged, not be hidden or auto-accepted. The same discipline now applies to material transfers, not only processing operations (`docs/data-model.md` §9).

The system must prevent, at the data-integrity level (not merely by convention or training):
- Double-counting material (the same physical quantity being counted as available in two places at once).
- Reuse of material that has already been consumed (i.e., allocating from an input that is already fully spoken-for).
- Recording reusable offcuts as waste, or vice versa, without an explicit reclassification action.
- Loss of the source relationship when material is transformed (every output must trace back to all of its input(s), even when there is more than one).
- Recording more output material than the available input, **unless** an explicit, documented adjustment accompanies the discrepancy (e.g., a measurement correction with a reason and responsible party).

This is the most safety-critical rule in the system and should be enforced structurally (e.g., database constraints, transactional invariants), not just in application logic.

### 2.6 Structural Purpose Tracking **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Every use of steel records both the structural element type and the specific reinforcement purpose.
- Structural element examples (non-exhaustive, extensible): columns, beams, slabs, foundations, staircases, retaining walls, shear walls, ramps, pile caps, and other construction elements.
- Reinforcement purpose examples (non-exhaustive, extensible): main reinforcement, longitudinal bars, top/bottom reinforcement, stirrups, ties, distribution reinforcement, additional reinforcement, vertical/horizontal reinforcement.
- **Structural element type and reinforcement purpose are two independent, separately configurable sets of master data.** Neither is a fixed enum tied to the example lists above, and neither is derived from or bound to the other — any purpose may in principle be associated with any element type, as configured per company/project.

### 2.7 Intended vs. Actual Usage **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Workers can declare an intended structural element, construction location, and reinforcement purpose to a supervisor before processing.
- Actual usage is recorded after processing/installation.
- Corrections are allowed when intended ≠ actual.
- All such changes are kept in an auditable history (who changed what, from what, to what, when).

### 2.8 Cutting and Bending **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Processing operations record: source material, input quantity/length, output quantities/dimensions, and responsible worker(s).
- One source bar can yield multiple output pieces.
- One output piece can be further processed into smaller pieces (recursive processing).
- Every resulting piece retains a traceable link to its source.
- Governed by the conservation rule in §2.5.

### 2.9 Reusable Offcuts and Waste **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
The system must distinguish, at all times, between:
- Material successfully used (installed or consumed in a completed operation).
- Reusable offcuts (not waste — eligible for allocation to another processing operation).
- Actual unusable scrap/waste.
- Material still available for processing (unallocated inventory).
- Material awaiting reconciliation (state/quantity not yet confirmed).

Key workflow that must be supported: offcuts from one operation (e.g., column bars) are commonly reused as input to a later, unrelated operation (e.g., beam reinforcement). An offcut is never automatically classified as waste — it must be explicitly registered as reusable inventory or explicitly written off as scrap.

Waste must be trackable at multiple rollup levels simultaneously: bundle level, processing-operation level, original-bar level (only where individual tracking exists), and project level.

Measurements must support pieces, length, and estimated weight, as appropriate to context.

### 2.10 Transportation **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT, generalized by PRODUCT DECISION]**
- Record dispatch from any inventory location and its destination: quantities, dispatch date, vehicle info (where applicable), responsible personnel. **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Record arrival and receiving confirmation at the destination, including partial receipt and discrepancy handling (`docs/data-model.md` §9). **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Dispatch is a distinct state from installation — dispatched/transferred material is not implicitly "installed." **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- **Must support material movement between any combination of locations**, including: warehouse → warehouse, warehouse → processing location, processing location → warehouse, warehouse → construction site, and processing location → construction site — not only warehouse-to-site. **[PRODUCT DECISION]** — the client's original ask described warehouse/processing-location dispatch to a destination; generalizing this to every location pair (modeled as one `MaterialTransfer` entity, `docs/data-model.md` §9) was this project's own design choice to avoid a narrower, special-cased model.

### 2.11 Installation Tracking **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Record actual installation: project, building, level/floor, zone, structural element, element reference (e.g., "Column C-12"), reinforcement purpose, quantity, and link back to source material.
- **One installation event may draw material originating from more than one bundle** (e.g., a column's main reinforcement assembled from pieces traced to three different bundles) — the link back to source material must support this, not assume a single source per installation. **[PRODUCT DECISION]** — identified as a necessary consequence of the multi-bundle-processing requirement during data-model design, not separately requested by the client.
- Construction-location hierarchy must be configurable per project (different projects may structure "where" differently).

### 2.12 Reporting (future phase, requirements noted now) **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
- Original bundle quantities
- Current inventory
- Material withdrawals
- Processing history
- Reusable offcuts
- Actual waste
- Consumption by diameter
- Consumption by structural element
- Consumption by reinforcement purpose
- Usage by construction location
- Full genealogy: bundle → installation
- All reports are scoped to a single company (§2.1); no cross-company aggregation unless/until that is explicitly requested.

### 2.13 Goods Receipt, Opening Stock, and Intake Quarantine **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's Rebar Stock Management specification and Inventory Control Excel template.
- The client's starting inventory — an **opening stock** of 102 bundles (~204 MT), recorded once as a starting balance — is operationally distinct from every bundle received afterward through a formal **Goods Receipt Note (GRN)** process. The system must distinguish `OPENING_STOCK` bundles from `GRN_PURCHASE` bundles rather than treating every registered bundle identically.
- A GRN-sourced bundle carries additional receiving data beyond the original label transcription (§2.2): a GRN reference, a PO reference, a delivery note number, vehicle info, a weighbridge ticket number, and a reference to the mill's Material Test Certificate (MTC).
- A GRN-sourced bundle also records its **actual weighbridge-measured weight** — distinct from a theoretical weight derived from piece count × bar length × catalog linear density (§2.15). The gap between the two ("rolling margin") is a quality/quantity signal, not an error to be hidden.
- Incoming material (opening stock or GRN) is not automatically usable: every bundle records an **initial receiving disposition** — accepted, on hold, or damaged/discrepant. A bundle received on hold or damaged/discrepant is **quarantined** — it must not be processed, installed, or allocated until explicitly released by an authorized reviewer, with the release itself an auditable event, never a silent status flip. See `docs/data-model.md` §18, `docs/physical-database-design.md` §4/§12.
- This reuses, rather than duplicates, the accepted/on-hold/damaged-discrepant vocabulary already built for `MaterialTransfer` receipts (§2.10) — the same disposition concept now also gates material entering the system for the first time.

### 2.14 Surface Type: Deformed vs. Plain Bars **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's structural BOQ/Rebar Schedule workbooks.
- The client's projects use both **deformed** (ribbed) bars, 8 mm–28 mm, the default/common case, and **plain** (smooth) 6 mm bars, used for precast-element stirrups/links and ribbed-slab mesh.
- Surface type is a physical property of the steel, recorded on the bundle and inherited immutably by every derived material unit — exactly like diameter, grade, and standard (`docs/data-model.md` §10, §19).
- Surface type must participate in the specification-compatibility check that gates combining material within one processing operation (§2.5): a deformed and a plain bar of identical diameter and grade are still not combinable — they are physically different products.

### 2.15 Rebar Catalog and Theoretical Weight **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT + PROPOSED]**
Extracted from the client's Inventory Control Excel template and BOQ workbooks.
- The client's paper/spreadsheet process already works from a standard catalog of rebar sizes (by diameter and surface type), each with a known standard bar length, a known linear density (weight per meter), and a reorder threshold in metric tons. That a reusable catalog of this kind is needed is a **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**; representing it as a dedicated, company-scoped master-data table rather than hardcoded constants is this project's own design choice **[PROPOSED]**.
- A company may set a catalog item's linear density either from the theoretical formula (`diameter_mm² / 162`, kg/m) or from a standard engineering lookup table (which can differ slightly from the formula at a few diameters: 6mm=0.222, 8mm=0.395, 10mm=0.617, 12mm=0.888, 14mm=1.208, 16mm=1.578, 20mm=2.468, 24mm=3.55 kg/m) — both are legitimate, company-configurable sources, never a system-imposed single truth.
- The catalog underpins two derived reporting figures: a unit's theoretical weight, and a bundle's rolling margin against its weighbridge-measured actual weight (§2.13). See `docs/physical-database-design.md` §14.

### 2.16 Bar Bending Schedules, Shape Codes, and Engineering Takeoff **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's structural BOQ/Rebar Schedule workbooks.
- The client's BOQ/Rebar Schedule workbooks are organized as **Bar Bending Schedules (BBS)** — engineering takeoff documents specifying, per structural element and reinforcement purpose: bar mark, diameter, surface type, grade, a standard **shape code** (the bent profile, e.g. a stirrup or an L-bar) with its defining dimensions, cut length, and how many bars are required per member and in total.
- The system must hold this engineering takeoff data natively — a BBS schedule and its constituent lines — rather than treating BBS as an external, disconnected paper/spreadsheet process.
- Shape codes (the standard bent-bar profiles and which dimensions each one uses) are **company-scoped configurable master data**, consistent with the existing rule against hardcoding structural element/reinforcement-purpose lists (§2.6, Development Rule §5.11).
- A work order, material reservation, or intended-usage declaration may **optionally** reference the BBS line it fulfills, so real activity can be traced back to the engineering takeoff that called for it — optional because not every real-world operation originates from a formal BBS line.
- **Multi-spec structural members never become a multi-spec processing operation.** A precast beam cage combining 6 mm plain stirrups with 12 mm/14 mm deformed main bars is modeled as **one `StructuralElementInstance` receiving several single-spec `InstallationLine` rows**, one per spec — `processing_operations` stays strictly single-spec throughout. This resolves §8 Q11 below without weakening the input-compatibility rule.

### 2.17 Physical Stock Count and Variance Reconciliation **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's Inventory Control Excel template.
- The client's process includes a periodic **physical stock count**: a count taken at a storage location, where each counted material unit's system-recorded quantity is compared against what was physically counted, and any variance is investigated and, where confirmed, recorded as a documented adjustment.
- This is the same "discrepancies must never silently disappear" discipline already required for processing operations and transfers (§2.5, §2.10), now extended to a **third** kind of reconciling event — a count independent of any single processing or transfer event.
- A stock count must record who counted, who verified, when, and, per counted unit, both the system and the counted figures — the variance itself, not just the corrected total, is a preserved, auditable fact.

### 2.18 Human-Readable Reference Codes and Storage Sub-Locations **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's Rebar Stock Management specification and Inventory Control Excel template.
- Client staff refer to processing operations and reusable remnants/batches by short, memorable codes (e.g. "ST-PROC-0021" for a cutting/bending operation, "ST-REM-0084" for a tagged reusable remnant) rather than internal database identifiers. The system must generate and display these alongside its internal IDs, and a tagged remnant/batch's QR code should be able to reference its human-readable code.
- The client's warehouse storage locations are further subdivided into **zones, racks, and bays**, and a sub-location may itself sit inside a parent storage location. This *storage* sub-location structure (where inventory physically sits before use) is separate from, and must not be confused with, the *installation* construction-location hierarchy (§2.11) for where steel ends up in the building.

### 2.19 Maker-Checker Approval and Supporting Document Attachments **[CLIENT REQUIREMENT]**
Extracted from the client's Rebar Stock Management specification.
- The client's processes for goods receipt, cutting/bending, and physical stock counts follow a **maker-checker** pattern: one person records the entry, a second, distinct person reviews and checks/approves it before it is treated as final. This confirms a pattern for *these specific record types*; it is not yet a complete statement of a company-wide role/permission matrix (§8 Q2 remains otherwise open).
- Processing operations, reclassification events (e.g. offcut → scrap), and conservation adjustments must each be able to record a checking/approving actor and timestamp, distinct from the original recording actor — extending the existing "every change is attributed" principle (§2.7) to a second, independent attribution for review/approval.
- The client's paper process generates supporting documents — MTC certificates, weighbridge tickets, delivery notes, signed physical count sheets — that must be attachable to the record they support, so the digital record and its paper evidence stay linked.

## 3. Proposed Architectural Decisions

These are this team's recommendations, not yet confirmed, and must not be implemented until approved.

- **[PRODUCT DECISION]** approach, **[PROPOSED]** mechanism — Multi-tenancy: the shared-schema-with-`company_id` approach itself was decided in this project's working sessions (§2.1) — it was never a verbatim client directive; the client's actual requirement is simply that one company's data stay private from another's. What remains proposed is the *enforcement mechanism*: row-level security (or equivalent) at the database layer, plus a relationship-level rule that every foreign-key relationship between two tenant-scoped rows must connect rows sharing the same `company_id` (making a cross-tenant reference structurally impossible, not just unlikely), with application-level authorization layered on top for role/permission checks within a company. See `docs/data-model.md` §11. A database-per-tenant or schema-per-tenant model remains possible later for a client requiring physical separation — a partitioning decision on top of this model, not a redesign of it.
- **[PROPOSED]** Material genealogy: model transformations and transfers as **edges in a bipartite graph** (material-quantity nodes connected via join rows to operation/transfer nodes), not a single parent-pointer tree — this is what allows one operation to consume multiple source bundles and produce multiple outputs, one material unit to be reused across multiple later operations, and an output's source attribution to be recorded at exact, known-partial, or (as a fallback only) pooled precision — while every derived unit still retains traceability to all of its actual sources, never more and never fewer. See `docs/data-model.md` §6. Current-state quantities are *derived* from this graph (an immutable initial quantity per unit, never overwritten) rather than stored as a separately mutable column — see `docs/data-model.md` §5 for the audit/atomicity mechanics this gives the audit-history requirements (§2.4, §2.7).
- **[PROPOSED]** Conservation enforcement: represent each transformation's outputs as rows that must sum, **on length (and weight when available) — not on piece count**, to the transformation's consumed input, enforced via a database check/trigger (or equivalent transactional invariant) at write time — not only validated in application code — per §2.5. The same reconciliation discipline (dispatched vs. received quantity, a reconciliation status, documented adjustments) applies to material transfers (`docs/data-model.md` §9).
- **[PROPOSED]** Material specification integrity: diameter/grade/standard are stored on `MaterialUnit` itself (inherited at creation, immutable), and a single processing operation's inputs are proposed to require matching specification — so no operation can produce a spec-ambiguous output. See `docs/data-model.md` §10; pending client confirmation as open question §8 Q11.
- **[PROPOSED]** Database: PostgreSQL. Rationale: this is a transactional, relational, integrity-critical domain (ledgers, genealogy graphs, multi-tenant isolation) that benefits from strong referential integrity, transactions, and row-level security support.
- **[PROPOSED]** Structural element types and reinforcement purposes are stored as **company-scoped configurable master-data tables** (not enums in code), per §2.6.
- **[PROPOSED]** QR codes generated server-side, encoding a reference to an internal bundle ID — no third-party QR dependency, no IDIS dependency.
- **[PROPOSED]** Rebar catalog and theoretical weight: a company-scoped `rebar_catalog_items` master-data table (diameter, surface type, default grade/standard, standard bar length, linear density, reorder threshold) drives two derived formulas — a unit's theoretical weight and a bundle's rolling margin against its weighbridge-measured actual weight — computed, never separately stored, consistent with the "no competing quantity column" principle already applied elsewhere. See §2.15, `docs/physical-database-design.md` §3a/§14.
- **[PROPOSED]** Engineering takeoff (BBS) integration: `bbs_schedules`/`bbs_lines` hold Bar Bending Schedule data natively, with an optional link from work orders, reservations, and intended-usage declarations back to the BBS line they fulfill — representing a confirmed client need (§2.16) as structured data rather than an external spreadsheet reference.
- Application framework, hosting platform, and frontend stack: **not yet decided.** No specific framework/platform should be treated as chosen.

## 4. Assumptions (pending client confirmation)

- **[ASSUMPTION]** No data is shared across companies by default, including reference/master data (e.g., standard diameter lists) — each company configures its own, even if that means duplicate setup effort. See §8 Q1.
- **[ASSUMPTION]** A company may still have multiple projects running concurrently, each with its own location hierarchy, but all scoped under that one company.
- **[ASSUMPTION]** "Explicitly documented adjustment" (§2.5) means a reasoned, attributed correction record (reason + responsible user), not a free-form override with no accountability. Exact workflow/approval requirements are unresolved — see §8 Q7.
- **[ASSUMPTION]** Individual-piece tracking, when used, is opted into per bundle or per processing operation (not a single global on/off switch) — exact trigger/UX unresolved, see §8 Q3.

## 5. Development Rules

1. Work in clearly defined phases: requirements → design → implementation. Do not jump ahead.
2. Document requirements and missing information before writing code.
3. Never invent client requirements; clearly separate confirmed requirements, proposed architectural decisions, assumptions, and open questions (§1 provenance note). Do not treat unresolved operational questions as finalized decisions.
4. Explain significant technical decisions before implementing them.
5. Do not oversimplify material tracking or waste accounting — this is the core value of the system.
6. Prioritize data integrity, tenant isolation, and full traceability over convenience.
7. Keep day-to-day worker-facing operations simple, even though the underlying model is rich.
8. Use professional, scalable, maintainable engineering practices.
9. Never present placeholder/stub functionality as complete.
10. Document important architecture and database decisions as they're made.
11. Keep the system configurable/general-purpose — no project-specific, building-type-specific, or company-specific hardcoding (incl. no hardcoding of structural element or reinforcement purpose lists).
12. Design for multi-company support from the start, even though the first deployment serves one client — do not add shortcuts that assume a single tenant.

## 6. Domain Concepts (glossary)

- **Company (tenant)**: the top-level isolation boundary. All projects, users, suppliers, inventory, operations, genealogy, waste records, and reports belong to exactly one company and are invisible to all other companies.
- **Bundle**: a physical unit of reinforcement steel as received from a supplier, identified by a manufacturer label. Receives one internal system ID (unique within its company) and one generated QR code.
- **Heat/casting number**: a manufacturer-assigned identifier for the production batch of steel, used for quality traceability back to the mill.
- **Withdrawal**: removal of material from warehouse inventory for processing or direct use.
- **Processing operation (cutting/bending)**: an event that consumes input material (bars/pieces) and produces output pieces with new dimensions; may be recursive (output can become input to a later operation). Subject to the conservation rule (§2.5).
- **Offcut**: leftover material from a processing operation that is physically usable but was not consumed by the operation's primary purpose. Defaults to "pending classification," not "waste."
- **Waste/scrap**: material explicitly determined to be unusable. Distinct from, and never inferred automatically from, "offcut."
- **Reconciliation**: the act of resolving the state/quantity of material whose disposition is not yet confirmed, and verifying that recorded outputs sum to recorded inputs (§2.5).
- **Traceability granularity**: the level at which identity is preserved through transformations — bundle, batch (default), or individual piece (optional). Chosen based on physical reality, never assumed, never retroactively upgraded.
- **Structural element**: the physical concrete member steel is installed into (column, beam, slab, etc.) — company-scoped configurable master data, independent of reinforcement purpose.
- **Reinforcement purpose**: the functional role steel plays within a structural element (main bar, stirrup, etc.) — company-scoped configurable master data, independent of structural element.
- **Intended vs. actual usage**: the planned destination/purpose for material declared before processing, versus what was actually realized after the fact; discrepancies are corrected with an audit trail, not silently overwritten.
- **Dispatch vs. installation**: dispatch = material left a location in transit; installation = material was physically placed into a structural element. Always distinct states.
- **Opening stock vs. GRN purchase**: a bundle's origin — the one-time starting inventory recorded at system launch, versus every bundle received afterward through a formal Goods Receipt Note process (§2.13).
- **Quarantine (intake hold)**: a bundle or material unit received `ON_HOLD`/`DAMAGED_DISCREPANT` that cannot be processed, installed, or allocated until explicitly released by an authorized reviewer (§2.13).
- **Surface type**: whether a bar is deformed (ribbed) or plain (smooth) — a physical property alongside diameter/grade/standard, inherited immutably and checked for compatibility like the others (§2.14).
- **Rebar catalog**: company-scoped master data listing standard rebar sizes with their standard length, linear density, and reorder threshold (§2.15).
- **Bar Bending Schedule (BBS) / shape code**: the engineering takeoff document specifying what bars, bent to what standard shape, are needed where and in what quantity (§2.16).
- **Physical stock count**: a periodic count of material physically on hand at a storage location, reconciled against system-recorded quantities, with any variance investigated and recorded (§2.17).
- **Maker-checker**: a two-person control where the person who records an entry is distinct from the person who reviews/approves it (§2.19).
- **Attachment**: a supporting document (MTC certificate, weighbridge ticket, delivery note, signed count sheet) linked to the record it evidences (§2.19).

## 7. Design Documents

## 8. Open Questions (awaiting client confirmation — not decided)

1. **Cross-company data sharing**: Is any reference/master data (standard diameters, grades, standards catalogs) shared across companies, or is every company's configuration fully independent even for common industry data? (Default assumption: fully independent — §4.)
2. **User roles and permissions**: What are the system's user roles (warehouse staff, site supervisor, project manager, company admin, possible cross-company super-admin for platform operators) and what can each do? **Partially confirmed** by the client's Rebar Stock Management specification: goods receipt, cutting/bending, and physical stock counts follow a **maker-checker** pattern — a recording role distinct from a checking/approving role (§2.19). Still open: the full role list and permission matrix beyond these three record types.
3. **Individual-piece tracking trigger**: How/when does a user opt into individual-piece tracking — per bundle at registration, per processing operation, or some other mechanism? Is it ever system-suggested based on heuristics, or always a manual choice?
4. **Physical QR usage**: Is a physical QR sticker printed and attached to bundles/processed output, or is the QR only ever shown/scanned on-screen within a facility?
5. **Existing systems**: Any existing ERP/accounting/procurement systems to integrate with or migrate data from, per company?
6. **Connectivity/offline**: Mobile/offline requirements for site or warehouse workers where connectivity may be unreliable?
7. **Reconciliation adjustment workflow**: Who is authorized to record an "explicitly documented adjustment" (§2.5) when output exceeds input, and does it require approval, or just attribution/reason at time of entry?
8. **Units/locale**: Metric only, or must the system also support imperial units? **Confirmed** by the client's BOQ/Rebar Schedule workbooks and Inventory Control Excel template: metric only, with rebar manufactured to standard **ES ISO 6935-2:2011**. This resolves the metric-vs-imperial question; it does not by itself resolve whether `standard` should ever become a constrained/validated value rather than free text (that remains a separate, unopened question).
9. **Company onboarding**: Who provisions a new company (platform operator only, or self-service signup)? Is there a billing/subscription dimension to multi-company support, or is that out of scope for SteelTrace itself?
10. **Cross-project resource sharing within a company**: Can inventory/offcuts be transferred between two projects belonging to the same company, or is inventory always scoped to a single project?
11. **Input compatibility rule**: `docs/data-model.md` §10 proposes a concrete default — a single processing operation's inputs must share identical diameter/grade/standard (now also surface type, §2.14), so outputs can never be spec-ambiguous. **Partially confirmed** by the client's structural BOQ/Rebar Schedule workbooks: the one real workflow that looks like a "mixed spec" case — a precast beam cage combining 6 mm plain stirrups with 12 mm/14 mm deformed main bars (e.g. shown as cage types PB1, PB2) — is **not** actually a mixed-spec processing operation; it is one `StructuralElementInstance` receiving multiple single-spec `InstallationLine` rows (§2.16). This confirms the single-spec-per-operation default needs no exception for the cage case; `processing_operations` remains strictly single-spec. Still open: whether any other, as-yet-undocumented workflow needs a genuine mixed-spec operation.
12. **Tolerance auto-acceptance**: is there a length/weight discrepancy threshold small enough to be auto-accepted without a human-recorded adjustment, or must every discrepancy, however small, be explicitly recorded? Now applies to both processing operations and material transfers.
13. **Weight reconciliation obligation**: is weight reconciliation ever mandatory (e.g., for certain grades/diameters/clients), or always best-effort/optional? **Partially confirmed** by the client's Rebar Stock Management specification: weighbridge weight reconciliation is **mandatory at two specific points** — at GRN receipt (the weighbridge-measured actual weight vs. the theoretical weight from piece count/length/catalog density, §2.13/§2.15) and at scrap disposal (weighing scrap leaving the system). Outside those two points (e.g. mid-process reconciliation of a cutting/bending operation), weight reconciliation remains best-effort/optional, per §2.5's original "weight where actual weights are available" framing — still open for any point not covered by these two confirmed cases.
14. **Partial-receipt resolution authority**: when a transfer's received quantity doesn't match the dispatched quantity, who is authorized to resolve/close the discrepancy, and does it require approval or just attribution/reason at time of entry?
15. **Source-attribution recording effort**: is recording exact/known-partial source attribution for a processing output (`docs/data-model.md` §6) an expected part of normal worker data entry, an optional step taken only when convenient, or something added later by a supervisor? This determines how often real operations end up at "pooled" precision in practice.
16. **Cross-project material use** — **[PROVISIONAL PRODUCT DECISION made]**: explicit, auditable reallocation (never implying physical movement) is built as the mechanism (`docs/physical-database-design.md` §5). Still open: **who** (which role) is authorized to perform a reallocation.
17. **Post-receipt verification step** — **[PROVISIONAL PRODUCT DECISION made]**: no mandatory QC step beyond the now-confirmed GRN/intake quarantine (§2.13); receipts and intake both support `ACCEPTED`/`ON_HOLD`/`DAMAGED_DISCREPANT` outcomes with an auditable release event (`docs/physical-database-design.md` §9/§11/§12), extensible to a future formal inspection workflow. Still open: whether a real workflow will eventually need more than a single release reason/event.
18. **Pooled-attribution estimates in reporting** — **[PROVISIONAL PRODUCT DECISION made]**: known candidate bundle *sets* may be shown; percentages are never fabricated; an estimate, if shown, must be clearly labeled and never touches authoritative tables (`docs/physical-database-design.md` §4 `material_unit_origins`, §10). Still open: whether site/office staff actually want estimates shown at all, versus always just the known set.
19. **Reservation granularity in practice** — **[PROVISIONAL PRODUCT DECISION made]**: whole-unit-only reservation is kept for the initial release; partial reservation is achieved via a split first (`docs/physical-database-design.md` §9 `material_splits`/`material_reservations`). Still open: whether this is sufficient for real workflows.
20. **Auto-allocation of unallocated stock**: still open whether company-wide stock should be automatically allocated to whichever project first consumes it, or always require a deliberate, separate action (`docs/physical-database-design.md` §5).
21. **Reallocation/approval authority for the new maker-checker record types** (§2.19): who is authorized to act as the "checker" for a processing operation, reclassification event, or conservation adjustment — a specific role, any role other than the maker, or a designated supervisor? Not yet specified by the client beyond confirming the pattern exists.
22. **Physical stock count cadence and authority** (§2.17): how often are counts taken, who is authorized to count vs. verify, and is a count ever mandatory (e.g., month-end) versus purely ad hoc?
